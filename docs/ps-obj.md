Title: Remote Sensing-Based Detection and Classification of Water Stress in Sugarcane Using SAR Imagery 

Problem Statement
Sugarcane is highly sensitive to water availability, and water stress at different growth stages can significantly affect crop growth, yield, and productivity. Conventional methods of identifying water-stressed sugarcane fields rely heavily on field surveys and manual observations, which are time-consuming, costly, and difficult to perform over large agricultural areas. Moreover, these methods may not provide timely, plot-level information about the severity of water stress or the actual water condition of the crop.
Therefore, there is a need to develop a remote sensing and GIS-based approach for timely identification, classification, and mapping of water-stressed sugarcane crops using Synthetic Aperture Radar (SAR) satellite imagery. The proposed approach will process SAR data to distinguish healthy and water-stressed sugarcane crops across the major growth stages—germination, tillering, grand growth, and maturity. The system will further classify crop water stress into different severity levels and generate plot-level GIS maps using geographical coordinates. Such maps can support field-level decision-making and facilitate the communication of water-stress information to farmers for improved irrigation and crop management.

Objectives
1. To acquire and preprocess SAR satellite imagery for identification of healthy and water-stressed sugarcane crops at different growth stages.
Collect suitable SAR data for the study area.
Apply necessary preprocessing techniques.
Analyze SAR characteristics associated with crop water conditions.
Identify healthy and water-stressed sugarcane across germination, tillering, grand growth, and maturity stages.
2. To develop a water-stress classification and plot-level GIS mapping system for sugarcane crops.
Classify sugarcane fields according to water-stress severity: Very Low, Low, Medium, High, and Very High.
Estimate/derive indicators related to crop water content or moisture condition from SAR observations.
Generate plot-level water-stress maps using GIS and geo-coordinates.
3. To develop a farmer-oriented information framework for communicating crop water-stress conditions and supporting irrigation decisions.
Integrate plot boundaries and relevant farmer/field information with the generated maps.
Provide location-specific information about the severity of water stress.
Develop a suitable mechanism for communicating water-stress information to farmers for timely irrigation and crop management.
